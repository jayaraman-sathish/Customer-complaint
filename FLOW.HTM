Customer Complaint & Feedback Management — application flow

How a consumer complaint travels from an active QR on the bottle to a closed, audited case: the seven stages, who owns each one, and the conditions that must be met before a case can move on.

Distillery / alcobev .NET · SQL Server 7 stages · 6 roles RCA & CAPA with review gate
01

Two sides of one system

The consumer never sees the internal console, and internal users never fill in the consumer form. Both sit on one database, which is what makes a complaint traceable from the bottle in someone's hand to the filler head that caused it.

02

The seven stages

A case sits in exactly one stage at a time. Each stage names one owner and one action; the console shows that owner as a “Waiting on” chip on the case and as a task in that person's My Work list, so a case is never parked with nobody responsible.

03

What blocks each move

Gates are enforced in the console, not left to discipline. The “Submit for approval” button stays disabled with a visible checklist until all three of its conditions are met.

New → ClassifiedCustomer Care Executive
  • Complaint or feedback decided
  • Category chosen from the master list
  • Severity set — this fixes the SLA clock
Classified → Under InvestigationCustomer Care Executive
  • Department selected
  • Assignee picked from users who can investigate in that department — Customer Care staff are not offered
Under Investigation → RCA & CAPAAssigned QA/QC or plant user
  • At least one investigation entry recorded, using any of the five tools
RCA & CAPA → Pending ApprovalAssignee, gated by the QA Manager
  • Root cause documented and classified as Process / Equipment / Material / External
  • RCA marked reviewed by the QA Manager — re-saving the RCA resets this
  • At least one corrective or preventive action raised with an owner and target date
Pending Approval → ResolvedQA Manager only
  • Customer-facing resolution written
  • Approved — or returned to RCA & CAPA for rework
Resolved → ClosedCustomer Care Executive
  • Customer confirmation recorded (or explicitly closed without it)
  • Closure email queued to the consumer
04

Inside stage 4 — RCA and CAPA

This is the only stage with an internal loop, and the one that carries the audit weight. The separation matters: the person who does an action never signs off its own effectiveness.

05

Who can do what

Permissions are attached to the role, not the person, and the console hides or explains every action a role cannot take rather than failing silently.

Management is deliberately read-only: dashboards, trends and MIS without the ability to alter a case. The System Administrator additionally holds master data — categories, the severity/SLA matrix, brands and SKUs, plants, users and notification templates.

06

Clocks, escalation and the audit trail

SeverityResponseResolutionHandling rule
Critical8 h48 hConsumer safety or product integrity risk. Containment review mandatory.
Major24 h120 hQuality deviation affecting saleability or brand perception.
Minor48 h240 hCosmetic or informational issue with no consumption risk.

The clock starts at classification, not at submission, so a case cannot be starved by leaving it unclassified — an unclassified case is itself the Customer Care queue's first task. Cases approaching their resolution SLA turn amber; breached cases turn red, auto-escalate to the QA Manager and are counted in the header of every screen.

Every state change is written to the audit trail with actor, timestamp and detail — registration, classification, assignment, each investigation entry, RCA, RCA review, each action raised, started, completed and verified, resolution, approval and closure. Entries are append-only; nothing in the case can be edited after the fact.

Batch and bottling-unit data are what let the dashboard answer the question a quality head actually asks: not “how many complaints this month”, but “is one filler line, one supplier lot or one contract bottler generating them”.

08

Outside Phase-1

Named here so the boundary stays explicit: WhatsApp Business integration; SMS gateway and charges; AI/ML, OCR, automated categorisation or predictive analytics; native Android or iOS apps; proprietary SAP connector development beyond the agreed standard interface; historical data cleansing or migration not agreed at scope sign-off; third-party licences, hardware, infrastructure and consumption-based services.
